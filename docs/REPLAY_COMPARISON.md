# Replay comparison: checking the Lua port against the original game

Method for making the Shadow port (`Scripts/shadow/`) behave like the original GameCube game: record the original, feed the recorded pad input to the port, compare the two. Findings go into `docs/STH2005_PLAYER_REFERENCE.md` (section 20 and following), labelled with their evidence.

## The loop
1. **Record the original** (user, Dolphin running the game): `.venv/bin/python tools/shadowLiveLog.py --pad 8056ED4C tools/<name>.csv`, Ctrl+C to stop. One row per game frame: position, velocity, behavior, and with `--pad` the pad slot at 0x8056ED4C (`pad_sx`, `pad_sy` raw s16 stick, `pad_fx`, `pad_fy` float stick, `pad_buttons`), and always the camera's eye and look-at point (`cam_eye_x/y/z`, `cam_target_x/y/z`, since 2026-10-06; empty cells if the camera cannot be reached). It overwrites its CSV, so never run it to read a log; analyse the CSV with python.
2. **Make a replay**: `python3 tools/makeReplay.py tools/<name>.csv Scripts/shadow/replays/<name>.lua`. It writes the stick as camera-relative forward/right floats, the A button, the start position, `heading0` and the camera heading `phi` per row: from the camera columns when the recording has them (2026-10-06), else from the rows holding the world stick vector, interpolated between them (see "Reading the camera"; the older `--camera stick|eye|dir` options are gone). `python3 tools/makeReplay.py tools/<name>.csv --check-camera [--ai]` compares the two on the rows holding both and writes nothing.
3. **Register it**: add `sf.include("shadow/replays/<name>.lua")` to `Scripts/shadow/main.lua` and the name to `ORDER` in `Scripts/shadow/replay.lua`. A missing generated file gives "cannot open Assets/Scripts/shadow/replays/<name>.lua": run step 2.
4. **Run the port** (user builds and runs; `cmake --build build` copies `Scripts/` to `build/Assets/Scripts`). Redirect the app's stdout to a file (Lua has no file output), e.g. `tools/port_run.txt`. R teleports Shadow to the recording's start and runs it: first R the first name of `ORDER`, second R the second, and round again. Each tick prints `REPLAY\t<name>\t<tick>\t<t>\t<x y z>\t<vx vy vz>\t<motion>`. `ShadowReplay.enabled = false` gives R back as a plain respawn.
5. **Compare**: `python3 tools/compareReplay.py tools/<name>.csv tools/port_run.txt <name> [step]`. Prints, per step (0.1 s), planar speed, vertical speed and height, original / port / difference, plus the behavior and motion names, the path drift (dx, dz and length of the port's horizontal distance from its start minus the original's, with the original's (x, z) turned into the port's axes: added 2026-10-05) and the largest differences. Only axis-independent quantities are compared, because the port's world is the original turned by `RenderWareYawDegrees` (hypothesis: (x, z) goes to (z, -x), heading h to h - pi/2).
   `--ai` prints a compact form instead (for Claude; added 2026-10-06): one summary line (largest differences, the first time each difference passes a bound, the stick reversals with their verdict), then one row per step with the original's values and the port's differences only; quiet stretches (planar under 3.5, one tick of acceleration; vy under 2 or at the known press; height under 1; drift moving under 1; facing under 2 degrees from planar 30) are collapsed to `t0-t1 ok`. 4 to 45 times shorter than the tables on the nine recordings of 2026-10-06.
   **Digest of one run**: `python3 tools/digestRun.py tools/<name>.csv [--from T] [--to T] [--every N]` (the original) or `python3 tools/digestRun.py tools/port_run.txt <name> [...]` (the port's last run of it): one line per frame with the decoded columns only (behavior, position from the start, velocity, planar and 3D speed, ground normal, facing, the behavior words 11 to 13, animation index, pad stick and A; for the port its heading, facing, turn rate, grounded, ground normal, wall flag and wall normal), both in the port's axes so the two line up. The original's duplicate rows are dropped and the raw memory dumps left out (about 40 times smaller than the CSV). The CSV and the REPLAY lines keep their full form: the dumps are the evidence, and makeReplay / compareReplay read them.
6. **Interpret, change the port, document**: decode the rule from the numbers, port it with a comment labelling the evidence, add a bullet to the reference doc, re-record or re-run. The user builds and runs; do not build or run the app, only the python tools on pasted/stored files.

## Reading the camera (so phi is not fitted)
**Without any camera address (observed 2026-10-05 on `live_accel` and `live_half`):** the logger already writes the player's world-space stick vector (`stick_x/y/z`, player+0xb8). The game zeroes it each frame after the Ground behavior reads it, so only 10 to 20 per cent of the rows hold it (none on `live_run`), but where it is set its length equals the pad stick's and its angle minus the pad stick's angle (forward = -pad_fy, right = +pad_fx, right sign +1 by the smaller spread) is the camera heading: 8 to -3 degrees on `live_accel` (mean error 1.4 degrees against 8 to 11 for the fitted phi), -2 on `live_half`. `makeReplay.py` interpolates it between those rows on recordings without the camera columns (the `--camera stick` flag it once needed is gone; 2026-10-06). Needs a recording with the stick held; no extra logging. The eye/dir address search below is only a fallback.

**2026-10-06: `makeReplay.py` no longer fits or searches anything.** The velocity-direction fit of phi, the search for the stick's right sign (it is +1, `pad_fx` right), and the `--camera eye|dir` readers (the camera address was never found) are removed. phi comes only from the rows holding the world stick vector with the stick held, interpolated linearly between them (hypothesis: smooth camera) and held at the ends; a recording that holds the stick without such a row is refused. Only the starting heading `heading0` (the first velocity direction) is still a guess. The `--find-camera` / `--camera ADDR` logging stays in `shadowLiveLog.py` (superseded the same day: removed, see below).

**2026-10-06, later: the camera is found (external, decompile of `main.dol`; checked on the RAM dump `shadowDecomp/mem1.raw`). Supersedes "the camera address was never found".** The world stick `+0xb8` is written by `Player::Control::UserInput` (`FUN_800a19a4` -> `FUN_800a13c4` -> command 1 -> `FUN_800a78fc`), which turns the pad stick by the yaw of d = camera `+0xa8` (look-at) - camera `+0x9c` (eye). The camera comes from the static camera manager at `0x8056ee14`: `+0x0c` is an array of 8 byte entries (count `+0x08`) whose first word is the camera, indexed by player `+0x54` (-1 -> 0). On the dump: camera 0 at `0x807d76a8`, look-at within 4 units of the player, eye 60 away (observed). Layout and the full chain: `docs/STH2005_PLAYER_REFERENCE.md`, "The camera and the stick's world direction".
- `shadowLiveLog.py` follows that chain on every poll and logs `cam_eye_x/y/z`, `cam_target_x/y/z` raw (no flag; `--find-camera` and `--camera ADDR` are removed). A change of the camera alone also writes a row.
- `makeReplay.py`: when the columns are present, phi per row = atan2(dz, dx) of target - eye over the original's x / z (the convention of the stick method: forward = (cos phi, sin phi)); a row with empty camera cells holds the last phi. Recordings without the columns keep the stick-vector method unchanged (the regenerated `live_accel`, `live_slopes`, `live_run`, `live_rotation` replays were byte-identical).
- Check: `makeReplay.py <csv> --check-camera` prints t, camera phi, stick-vector phi, difference, 3D speed, ground normal y and "fast" (`+0xa0` bit 0, s3 >= 250) on every row holding both; `--ai` gives one summary line (count, mean and max |difference| over all rows, over slow flat rows, and with the camera taken one frame earlier) and only the rows more than 1 degree apart, quiet stretches as `t0-t1 ok (n)`. Expected (hypothesis): slow flat rows agree within about 1 degree; fast slope rows differ (the game rotates the stick into the ground basis there, and that depends on the camera's pitch too); if the "one frame earlier" mean is clearly the smaller, the stick uses the previous frame's camera and makeReplay should shift phi by a frame. Not measured yet: no recording has the columns.
- `digestRun.py` shows the camera heading as `cam` (port axes, degrees) when the recording has the columns.

## Known limits and constants
- Alignment: `frame_times` (in `makeReplay.py` and `compareReplay.py`) is the wall clock plus the frames Dolphin ran inside one poll. Observed on `live_run`: the jump's first speeds (vy 100, 97.52, 95.08) were read under 8 ms apart, so game time ran about 1.5 frames ahead of the wall clock there, and the port's jump was 2 frames out of step. A chain of three or more rows each under 8 ms apart with a changing (planar speed, vy) adds its extra frames to every later row. Tried and dropped: counting frames from position or velocity changes (it counted 5 to 8 per cent too many on `live_accel` / `live_half`, whose 127 distinct speed steps match 126 wall-clock frames, and the port then ran ahead of the original by 10 to 40 speed units: measured 2026-10-05). The wall clock alone matched the running recordings. Hypothesis: 60 frames a second. Still open: a frame in which nothing changes while moving writes no row. Rebuild every replay after changing it.
- The port's rest vertical speed is -11.34 (ground press), the original stores 0: a known constant difference.
- Spawn: the port's ground lies 5.02 below the recorded height whatever the spawn height (cause unknown); `START_LIFT = -5.0` in `replay.lua`. The compare tool takes the port's height at `SETTLE_TIME` (0.25 s), after the drop.
- Tick rate 60 Hz is a guess.
- The camera heading `phi`: on recordings with the camera columns (from 2026-10-06) it is read on every row; on older ones it is only known at the rows holding the world stick vector (10 to 30 per cent of the rows) and interpolated between them. On fast slopes (s3 >= 250) the game turns the stick in the ground's basis, which a horizontal phi cannot rebuild exactly (external; see the reference).
- Stick reversals: a stick flipped to (almost) exactly the opposite way leaves the heading about 180 degrees from the stick, and a sub-degree heading difference picks the turn side (live_jumpdash_stick 1.66 s: the original turned +69 degrees, the port -63, then the jump dash went 100 degrees apart and the port fell into a pit). `compareReplay.py` lists every row where the pad stick turns 170 degrees or more and whether both turned the same way over 0.05 s; after one marked SIDES DIFFER the comparison is not evidence. When recording, avoid flipping the stick straight through the centre to the opposite side; sweep it round, or release it for a few frames first.
- Rules fitted to a recording are only validated by a different recording. Prefer new, independent recordings (other stick levels, turns, slopes, landing with the stick held, air/jump with analog stick) over re-checking the same one.

## Recording protocols used so far
- `live_run`: run, jump, dash, landing, jump from a slope.
- `live_accel`: full stick, accelerate, release, brake.
- `live_half`: stick held at about quarter, half and slightly more, then released (analog stick rule).
- `live_turn`: full-stick turns at speed 160 to 410, restarts, and slow-walk flicks (45 to 85). Result: the heading turn matches, the port loses the speed in a sharp turn (the original holds its current speed at speeds above 150, see section 20 of the reference). Mining all four CSVs found no sharp-turn sample between planar 86 (drops) and 150 (holds), so the threshold needs a recording of sharp turns, 90° and 180° off the heading, at planar speeds of 90 to 150.
- `live_flick`: one run from rest with a slow stick sweep to 90 degrees at about 316 and a skid to a stop (no sharp turn in the 86 to 150 band: the hold-or-drop threshold is still open; the skid is in section 20).
